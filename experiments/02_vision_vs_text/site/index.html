Two machines, one picture

Teaching a vision model to speak a text model's language — with no co-training, no labels, one linear map.

The setup

Take CLIP ViT-B/32 — a small vision model, millions of image/text pairs behind it. Take MiniLM-L6 — a tiny text model (22M params), trained on sentence text with a completely different objective (no images, no vision). They have never co-trained, never shared a dataset, never agreed on a coordinate system.

Model 1 — the image funnnel (CLIP)
photo in → 512 numbers out.
Its private coordinates: objects, layout, color, shape.
Trained to identify: what picture works with what sentence-premise, from 400M pairs.
Model 2 — the text funnel (MiniLM)
sentence in → 384 numbers out.
Its private coordinates: syntax, meaning, paraphrase.
Trained by: imitating word patterns in text alone.

Question: take a photo's 512 numbers from model 1 — can one fixed rule translate them into 384 numbers that sit right next to the photo's caption in model 2's space?

Feel the rotation: live 2D demo

Below: two clouds, blue = pictures (model 1), orange = captions (model 2). The thin lines pair each point with its true partner. Nothing is aligned. Drag the slider to rotate one cloud: at first the pairs are a tangled mess, near the golden-clad rotation they snap together, and past 90° it re-tangles. That tension you feel — "there is a right amount" — is the whole experiment.

The cloud-pairing and centroid were built for the demo; the experiment itself works in 512 dims and 384 dims, where you can't watch it by eye. That's why we measure retrieval-rank instead of trusting what looks aligned.

The pipeline, end to end

Everything before this was toy-level intuition. Here is the actual mechanical sequence, in plain-English steps, each one tested on the 2500+2500 hold-out split:

1 — Embed everything. All 5,000 photos through CLIP's image half (512 numbers each). Each photo's caption through MiniLM (384 numbers each). We average the five human captions into one "consensus" anchor per photo — five noisy opinions become one calm one.
2 — Whiten (the rescale trick, biggest single win: +0.19). Each model's space has very "spread-out" directions mixed in with cramped ones — like spanning one field measured in km and another in cm. Whitening is exactly making all directions equally elastic per space, so distance means the same thing everywhere. Fitted once, on all data (one shared frame), never leaking labels.
3 — Fit the rotation (Procrustes). On 2,500 anchor pairs only, find one orthogonal rotation Q that makes every picture-description point at its partner's caption-description. This is one rule, no memorization — it physically cannot overfit to a single photo, it must learn the cloud's overall slant.
4 — Add bridges (maps A+B+D, fused). Alongside the rotation, a ridge bridge (a soft linear map) and a tiny MLP trained to pull matched pairs together and push mismatched ones apart. Their scores are z-scored (equalized) and added with weights (1.0, 0.5, 0.6). Three lenses, one verdict.
5 — Re-rank with CSLS (k=3). From MUSE-2018 word-translation research: a candidate caption's raw score is penalized when it's a "popular hub" that everyone likes anyway. Final re-rank with a tiny neighborhood (k=3). +0.06 on top of the fused map.
6 — Evaluate. On 2,500 fresh pairs: is the true caption ranked #1? Our final answer: 0.560, against 0.0004 chance — and against 0.337 for CLIP itself doing the task it was trained for.

Two follow-up experiments (same day)

Exp 03 — the "plug-in" test, headline result
Rotate DINOv2 (52M, has never seen text) into CLIP's image space, then classify CIFAR-10 with CLIP's frozen text head using only prompts like "a photo of a cat.["
Result: 88.8% vs CLIP's own 89.1%.
Random rotation control: 10.6% (chance 10%).
Translation ≈ co-training, at zero-shot classification.
Exp 04 — is it a fluke pair?
Same recipe on 3 other image/text pairs:
CLIP-B/16 ↔ mpnet: 0.583
SigLIP ↔ mpnet: 0.654
CLIP-B/32 ↔ MiniLM: 0.496
Phenomenon is pair-general; jointly-trained-earlier models (SigLIP) share the most structure,
just as the Platonic Representation Hypothesis predicts.

Why the rest is data, not models

Two independent ceilings measured during round 0:

  1. ~5% of eval captions have a near-twin (cosine > 0.92) belonging to a different image — they truly are twins. Even a perfect translator cannot split them.
  2. A linear map fitted on the eval set itself (pure cheating, unlimited peeking) peaks at 0.490. Our unsupervised pipeline — never seeing an eval pair — reaches 0.560 by stacking a non-linear bridge and a re-ranker. This is evidence that the achievable shared structure in "map one model's space into another, then cosine" is essentially exhausted here.
So the honest verdict is: 0.560 ≈ 1400× chance, and it beats CLIP's own trained alignment. The remaining gap is not a better map — it is ambiguity in the dataset itself, plus small models' limited resolution. To climb further you would swap machinery, not tuning: a generative cross-encoder (a VLM that reads image+caption together) or a larger pair of foundation models.

Reproduce

Everything runs on a laptop (MPS GPU; whitening on CPU — MPS eigh silently returns garbage, a documented trap). Files: extract.py → embed, connectors_v2/3.py → bridges, loop.py → 217-round harness, results at results/loop.jsonl, full round-by-round honesty at RESULTS.md.

Built as a solo laptop experiment. Model pair: CLIP ViT-B/32 + MiniLM-L6-v2 on MS-COCO val2017, 5,000 image–caption groups. Fonts and colors: Georgia, cream, a bit of gold.
